## Executive summary

The recommended lecture is **Human-Centered AI Needs Answerable Institutions**. Population aging is not principally a retirement story; it is a redesign of functional ability, care capacity, workforce and institutional responsibility. The physician’s contribution is to demonstrate that a technically correct output is insufficient when no one can explain, challenge, repair or sustain the care pathway around it.

The strongest business direction is not generic “aging AI.” It is AI embedded in a paid, staffed care process: dementia-care operations, primary-care function/transitions, home-health quality workflows, and evidence-generation infrastructure for narrowly defined digital measures. Sarcopenia/frailty and care robotics are important, but AI largely supplies measurement and triage; the outcome-backed core remains exercise, nutrition, multifactorial assessment, human response and longitudinal follow-through.

## Contradictions

- Human-centred AI is not obsolete; it needs operational conditions. Answerability is the proposed operating model.
- High discrimination, clearance or engagement does not establish clinical utility, equity or workforce substitution.
- Care robots may help bounded tasks and some engagement outcomes; evidence does not justify replacement of relationships or direct-care workforce.

## Final recommendation

Open with the abandonment question, establish the demographic/care-capacity pressure, show bounded medical evidence, explain paid care-pathway opportunities, then introduce four answerability disciplines and a 0–6 evidence ladder. Close by asking leaders to name one AI pathway whose owner, limits, challenge and repair they will make explicit.
